SUPO on CodeGym, Qwen3.5-9B

Does summarization-based context management let a 4K-context policy match a 32K-context GRPO policy on CodeGym tool-use tasks? With Qwen3.5-9B: it closes most of the gap, learns to summarize and to use fewer tool calls, but does not reach parity in 100 steps.

Result. GRPO-32K: 0.766 → 0.867. SUPO-4K×8: 0.633 → 0.758 (best 0.828 at step 70; mean of the last three evaluations 0.786 vs 0.867). The paper's +3.2-point SUPO advantage does not reproduce at this model size; its secondary claims do (summaries are used, overruns drop 12% → 5%, ~35% fewer tool calls).

Setup

policyQwen3.5-9B, non-thinking; FSDP2 actor + vLLM 0.24 rollouts (8 replicas, TP=1); 1 node × 8 H100 per arm
dataCodeGym (HF VanishD/CodeGym, EN). Train: 12,800 tasks, ≤ 2 per seed problem, oracle ≤ 80 calls. Eval: 128 tasks from 128 unseen seeds whose oracle needs 40–90 calls. Prompts ≤ 1,536 tokens. The paper's own lists were never released; the split follows its stated rules.
optimisation100 steps = 1 epoch; 128 prompts × 8 rollouts per step; mini-batch 32; 1 PPO epoch; lr 1e-6; clip 0.20 / 0.28; token-mean loss; no KL, no entropy bonus; supo advantage (identical to GRPO when there are no summaries)
episode≤ 100 LLM calls; ≤ 1,024 tokens per turn; 10 consecutive turns without a call → overlong; observations as user turns; reward = CodeGym Done verifier
GRPO 32Kworking context 32,768 (prompt 2,048 + response 30,720); no summaries; fused log-prob kernels + optimizer offload for memory
SUPO 4K×8working context 4,096 (prompt 4,096 + response 5,120 per trajectory); ≤ 7 summaries → 32K effective; summary turn ≤ 1,024 tokens
evaluationgreedy pass@1 on the 128 held-out seeds before training and every 10 steps

Reading. Both arms improve substantially (+10 and +12.5 points). With a 9B policy the 4K×8 arm does not reach the 32K arm: the gap narrows from 13 points at step 0 to 5–11 points over the last 30 steps. What does reproduce from the paper: the policy learns to use summaries (1.3 per episode), its context overruns fall from 12% to 5%, and it reaches its accuracy with ~35% fewer tool calls than GRPO. Two caveats in SUPO's favour: the 9B base model is already strong with a long context (0.77 before training), leaving less headroom for a context-management method; and SUPO's curve is still noisy and rising at steps 70–90 while GRPO's is flat, so a longer schedule may close more of the gap. Absolute numbers are not comparable with the paper (different policy family and size, a reconstructed eval split, and a prompt-length cap).

Roughly 2 × 8 H100 × 24 h of training, plus ~30 GPU-hours of shakeout runs.

What broke on the way

  1. Pods run NVIDIA driver R535 (CUDA 12.9 API); verl HEAD's cu130 stack cannot initialise → venv rebuilt on cu129 and flash-attn 2.8.3 compiled in-pod (no cu12 / torch-2.11 wheel exists).
  2. 32K-token PPO updates OOMed at 74 GB → fused linear + log-prob kernels and optimizer offload for the GRPO arm.
  3. At 1,024 rollouts per step the vLLM engine died silently twice: FlashInfer JIT-compiled Qwen3.5's linear-attention kernels with nvcc at first use (memory-cgroup kills of cicc) → prebuilt triton backend, FlashInfer sampler off, staggered sandbox spawns, execute-model timeout raised.
  4. Two agent-loop bugs only visible at scale: missing weight-version tags, and zero-token trajectories (post-summary cut-offs, plus one environment whose code does not parse under Python 3.12 — the official CodeGym server pins 3.11.2).
  5. Checkpoints are 113 GB; HDFS fuse writes from the pod ranged 9–125 MB/s and the mirror's verify raced verl's local rotation. Complete checkpoints on HDFS: GRPO steps 35 and 95, SUPO step 40. Both arms were resumed once from HDFS after the step-50 crash (item 4).
